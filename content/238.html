Virtual Battles

Published 02-15-2011



Off-line flying in IL-2 is enjoyable and entertaining. It's possible to spend untold hours doing battle against computer generated and control AI (artificial intelligence) opponents. One can fly individual missions, entire campaigns, or exercise one's creative talents in designing one's own missions and campaigns without ever exhausting the possibilities afforded by the simulation. The real enjoyment of the sim though, to my way of thinking, is realized when dozens and dozens of like-minded virtual combatants join together in an on-line battle. One sees that in places like HyperLobby, where fans of the on-line experience congregate to display their skills against one another.

Repeatedly flying an individual mission, however enjoyable, inevitably becomes, if not boring, certainly predictable. Likewise, flying in a "free-for all" server on HyperLobby, or for that matter in a co-op mission in the same place, if repeated enough times, becomes predictable as well.

And, unfortunately, when one flies online in an open venue like HyperLobby, sooner or later one runs across a competitor or other virtual pilot (call him or her what you will, the label isn't particularly important) who has a different view of what is "fun" or "fair" than do you. Occasionally the experience can become so negative that the fun and entertainment value disappears, remaining only for the "other fellow", so to speak.

Enter the world of squadron flying, and the world of the online virtual combat arena. Some folks are wary of joining a squadron; after all, what will others think of our flying ability? Overcoming an initial fear of being judged is easy, though. And, assuming the pilot selects a compatible group of squad mates, the world of on-line virtual combat opens wide and beckons the new combatant to experiences beyond a wild imagination.

Squadron flying, most would agree, offers the opportunity to fly with other folks of a like mind; it is more likely that squad mates share a similar view of what conduct is fair, entertaining, and fun. The "competitor" one occasionally sees in an open server venue like HyperLobby becomes a rarity, substituted with people who rapidly become "virtual friends".

Squadrons tend to share common views, and many squadrons are alike. When two or more get together in a virtual combat arena, the richness of the IL-2 experience increases many-fold. One particular ability has been lacking from the onset in IL-2 though; the ability to have a "continuing battle" where, between missions, the effects of the previous mission transfer and translate into the next such that accomplishments and failures of pilots and their squads carry over from mission to mission, eventually helping to determine the ultimate outcome of the "war".

Early on, before the advent of "mods", there were several efforts to accomplish these goals, chief among them perhaps was the Scorched Earth Online War (SEOW). SEOW uses a scripting engine to control the battlefield and to track the results of a previous mission, factoring them into the creation of the subsequent mission. The 352nd Virtual Fighter Group was among early SEOW participants and our own 352nd Oscar was involved in the testing and development of SEOW. SEOW was built on the co-op mission construct of IL-2 and hence had the strengths and weaknesses of the co-op; if a pilot died or bailed or crashed in the mission he was out for the duration. While that reality may have tended to make a pilot more cautious and more inclined to think about surviving, it lessened the overall enjoyment and playability of the on-line war.

With the mods came Zuti's Dogfight Server mod, and the ability to layer the virtues of the co-op mission concept onto a dogfight map; the pre-planning and scripting possibilities of a co-op mission were now available in the dogfight world; dying no longer prevented a player from returning to the battle and enjoyment was necessarily enhanced.

Scripts have been developed which control the DS Server mod in such a way that, like SEOW in the co-op world, the actions of a side in one mission affect the conditions of the start of the next mission; a sequence of missions then becomes a varying and "living" battlefield. Battlefield planners can pre-plan the next mission's force movements, and the pilot's actions then have an accumulating effect on the battle until the conclusion of the war. It's a flexible model with, if not infinite, vast possibilities. Throw in squadron participation with folks who are fun and fair and the entertainment value of the Virtual Online War becomes enormous.

The 352nd Virtual Fighter Group has participated in virtual on line wars for many years, in our own SEOW server battles, in the former Clash of Titans, Forgotten Skies, and now in the AKAFA Online Virtual Wars. Virtual Wars can present their own issues, however; different people have different ideas of how competition should be, what "fair" is, and how to deal with the inherent dissimilarities of various aircraft. Differences of this type can, if not recognized and controlled, destroy the experience. Clash of Titans and Forgotten Skies both suffered such a fate.

Read more on this issue in the 'AKAFA' page.
