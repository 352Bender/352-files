Savannah Reunion - Hanger

Published 01-22-2013



October, 2004 Monday at the Hanger



Savannah is a lovely city, unlike any other I have ever visited. There are parks and squares in nearly every block, stately homes grace street after street, and the riverfront simply "drips" history. It was a perfect setting for the 352nd Fighter Group 2004 Reunion. The weather, however, did not accomodate the gathering. Late summer saw a series of hurricanes bear down on the southern coast leaving constant wet weather behind. The four days of the Gathering were constantly overcast, occasionally rainy, and always gray.

One of the side effects of the weather was the inability of several warbirds to arrive for the Gathering -- blue nosed Mustangs and a flying P-47 were scheduled to attend but were unable to arrive because of the poor weather. However, attendees were graced with the presence of three absolutely beautiful blue nosed Mustangs; Moonbeam McSwine, Hell-er Bust, and the truly stunning Cripes a'Mighty.

There were two other P-51s there also, in the livery of other fighter groups. Wandering among the Mustangs one couldn't help but be awed by what they represented. Their presence among the gathered pilots, crew, and support personnel added to the beauty of the moment. These were truly once-in-a-lifetime experiences.

A group of veteran pilots, including Leonard Travis, Clint Haynes (in the white hat),
Bobby Dodd (in the red beret), Don Bryan, Bob Reese,and Ray Mitchell
standing in front of Cripes A'Mighty





Don Bryan told of his "ritual" he performed before every
mission .... he would pee on the rear wheel of his fighter
for good luck. For the assemblage he demonstrated his
ritual. No, no, no, only partly!!





Ray Mitchell and Cripes A'Mighty

Cripes a'Mighty is a fabulously restored copy of Maj. George Preddy's ride. So careful
was the restoration that the hue of the blue paint on the nose was reportedly determined
by visiting the former 352nd Fighter Group's base in Bodney, England and searching for
remnants of the blue paint used to color the noses of the group's Mustangs. The detail on
this aircraft is simply stunning to behold.






Al Barrows examining a gun bay

Cripes A'Mighty had 50 caliber machine guns mounted in her wings
in the correct locations, and bands of ammunition packed into the
ammo bays demonstrating what the aircraft looked like when fully armed
and ready for combat. The gun bays were detailed right down to the metal
labels attached to the hatch covers.






Jim Wood and Moonbeam McShine






Mrs. Heller standing in front of
Hell-er Bust

Her husband, Edward Heller, was the pilot of this famous plane and a
veteran of the 352nd Fighter Group. He only recently passed away and
was sorely missed.






Mrs. Heller, CO Deacon, and Hell-er Bust






Charlie Agee and Hell-er Bust

Charlie Agee was the crew chief of Hell-er Bust. Here he stand before the
replica of his plane, grinning from ear to ear. Notice his name stenciled
on the canopy cowling.
